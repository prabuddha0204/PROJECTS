CURIE TEMPERATURE ALARM PROJECT

📚 THEORY

The Curie temperature is a critical point in the study of ferromagnetic materials, marking the temperature at which the material loses its permanent magnetic properties and becomes paramagnetic. This occurs because, at temperatures above the Curie point, the thermal energy overcomes the forces holding the magnetic moments of the atoms in alignment, causing them to become randomly oriented.

Materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt exhibit ferromagnetism below their Curie temperatures. Once they reach or exceed the Curie temperature, their magnetization is lost, and they can no longer generate a magnetic field. This phenomenon is fundamental to many applications in electronics, magnetic storage, and even in the design of magnets for industrial uses.

📝 HOW TO USE

This simulation demonstrates how different materials lose their magnetic properties when heated beyond their Curie temperature. Follow these steps to explore:

  1. Choose a Material: From the dropdown menu, select a material (Nickel, Iron, Steel, Copper, or Aluminum). Each material has a unique Curie temperature.
  2. Adjust the Temperature: Use the temperature slider to manually increase the temperature of the material, or click "Start Heating" to simulate automatic heating. As the temperature rises, observe the material's behavior.
  3. Watch the Magnet: As the temperature approaches the Curie point, the material will lose its ability to magnetize the magnet. The weight will drop once the Curie temperature is exceeded.
  4. Experiment: Try heating different materials and compare their behavior at various temperatures. Can you identify the temperature at which each material loses its magnetism?

Once you reach the Curie temperature, the material's magnetism will disappear, and the weight will fall. This demonstrates how temperature impacts the magnetic properties of materials!
